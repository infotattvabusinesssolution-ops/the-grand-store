For an e-commerce site such as Grand Store, the PostNet integration should feel like a simple delivery service built into checkout, not like a separate courier system.
The key principle is:
The customer chooses where they want the order delivered, sees the delivery cost and expected timing clearly, and PostNet handles the logistics in the background.
Recommended Checkout Flow
1. Customer reaches Checkout
After reviewing the cart:
CART → CHECKOUT → DELIVERY → PAYMENT → ORDER CONFIRMATION
At the top, show a simple progress indicator:
① Delivery Details → ② Delivery Method → ③ Payment → ④ Confirmation
This immediately gives the customer confidence about where they are in the process.

11. Very Important for a Multi-Vendor Grand Store
Because Grand Store is a multi-vendor marketplace, I would not make PostNet integration directly customer → PostNet.
Instead:
CUSTOMER
      ↓
GRAND STORE ORDER
      ↓
ORDER SPLIT BY VENDOR
      ↓
VENDOR A ──→ PostNet Shipment A
VENDOR B ──→ PostNet Shipment B
VENDOR C ──→ PostNet Shipment C
      ↓
      GRAND STORE
      ↓
ONE CUSTOMER ORDER
The customer should ideally still see:
Order #GS102458
rather than becoming confused by three different courier orders.
Behind the scenes, however, Grand Store maintains:
Master Order ID 
Vendor Order ID 
Shipment ID 
PostNet Waybill Number 
Tracking Number 
Parcel Number 
Weight 
Dimensions 
Shipping cost 
Vendor responsible 
Delivery status

Don't show:

The buyer should see only:

One additional feature I strongly recommend
Give the customer a "Change Delivery Method" option right up until payment.
So if they initially select:
Home Delivery — R120
they can easily change to:
PostNet Collection — R80
without restarting checkout.
This creates a much more polished experience and can also reduce delivery costs.
